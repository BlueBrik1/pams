# Strongest fair baseline for exact second-order masked ciphertext verification

## Published exact methods considered
| method | algorithm | nonlinear work per coefficient | verdict for a 3-share hardware lane |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bos et al. 2021 (TCHES 2021(4), Alg. 2) | two range checks w = x + 2^11 − S(c), x − E(c) (mod q), each **MSB of a prime-modulus A2B** | 2 × A2B mod q (12-bit, with modular reduction) + AND | dominated: two prime-modulus conversions (Coron et al. 2023 make the same observation) |
| Coron et al. 2023 (TCHES 2023(1)) | for d = 10/11: products ∏(x − x̃_i) mod q over ≤ 4 candidates (SecMultList), then zero test; for d = 4: HOCompress (modulus switching) | masked mod-q multiplications (each 9 modular multipliers + 36 random bits at 3 shares) | dominated in hardware area at 1 coefficient/cycle |
| **Fritzmann et al. 2022 / D'Anvers et al. 2023 "Simple"** (exact; the best exact method in the 2023 comparison) | modulus switch to 2^{d+t} per share, t = 13 for 3 shares; subtract the scaled public value; one power-of-two A2B; OR of the top d bits | one (d+13)-bit A2B + d-bit zero test | **baseline** |
| probabilistic (D'Anvers 2022 random sum; GF method; Coron 2023 multiplicative) | — | — | excluded: nonzero false-acceptance probability (exact contract) |
| X2X (Norga et al. 2025) | improved A2B/B2A gadgets for d+1 shares | — | full text inaccessible. A better conversion gadget would apply to the power-of-two additions of **both** lanes (baseline 24-bit, PAMS 14-bit); it is not a different decision algorithm |
| Dobiáš et al. 2026 | first-order integrated verification (FPGA) | — | not a matched second-order ASIC baseline |

## Implemented baseline (`rtl/base.v`, generated by `models/gen.py base`): strongest exact Simple variant
- **Mathematics:**
  - y_i = ⌊x_i 2^{13+d}/q⌋, computed as an exact ⌊x · 2^24/q⌋ followed by ≫ (11 − d);
  - Z = Σ y_i − (2c − 1) 2^12 + 1 (mod 2^{13+d});
  - decision = [the top d bits of Z are zero].
  - Exact for all inputs (MATHEMATICS §3), at the minimal t = 13 *for this rule*; the optimal offset A = 1 was found exhaustively.
- **Optimizations given to the baseline:**
  - minimal exact multiplier constant (smallest shift, searched);
  - mode shift instead of mode-dependent carry taps (fewer gadgets);
  - CSA with one gadget per majority;
  - ripple carry only into bit 13;
  - **carry-free zero test of the top region** (parallel OR gadgets, so no carry ripple through the top bits);
  - early OR tree, so that only one top bit waits for the carry;
  - automatic minimal pipeline alignment.
- **Security:** identical gadget (HPC2) and composition argument to the candidate (SECURITY.md).
- **Randomness:** 3 bits per gadget per cycle, 57 gadgets → **171 bits/cycle**.
- **Architecture:** 1 coefficient per cycle (initiation interval 1), latency 31 cycles to the per-coefficient decision, plus 4 cycles for the ciphertext decision. It supports all of ML-KEM-512/768/1024 (d ∈ {4, 5, 10, 11}), selectable per coefficient.

## Common implementation contract (baseline and candidate)
- **Inputs per cycle:** x_0, x_1, x_2 (12-bit canonical shares), c (11-bit), mode (d), vin / first / last; rnd (fresh bits).
- **Outputs:**
  - per-coefficient decision shares (test visibility only);
  - the whole-ciphertext decision sharing, valid 4 cycles after the last coefficient's decision.
- **Accumulator:** one HPC2 gadget with a 2-cycle feedback (two interleaved chains) plus a final HPC2.
- **Technology and settings:** Nangate45 typical; Yosys + ABC flow B (BUF_X1 drive, 5 fF load); HPC2 `keep_hierarchy`; OpenSTA data-path groups (in2reg, reg2reg, reg2out, in2out) with I/O at the clock edge.
- **P&R:** the project's OpenROAD flow (pnr/run_pnr.sh, pnr.tcl), period target 2.0 ns, density 0.6, utilizations 45/50/55%.

## Unimplemented or unverified assumptions
- Randomness generation is modelled by Trivium-64 instances (seeding/TRNG excluded).
- No X2X-style conversion gadgets (full text inaccessible); see the table above.
- The baseline is our implementation of the published algorithm, not a published circuit. No published second-order ASIC implementation of an exact ML-KEM verifier was available for direct comparison.
